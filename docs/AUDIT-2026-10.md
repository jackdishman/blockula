# Blockula protocol v1: internal security review and fix rounds (October 2026)

**Publication note, October 18, 2026.** This is the record of an internal security review of
Blockula protocol v1 and of the fix rounds that followed it. It was run by AI models: Claude Fable
5.1 and Claude Opus 5.5, with a Codex session continuing the integration and verification. **It is
not a third-party audit.** No human security firm took part, and the external audits remain gates
for mainnet (SEC-1 and SEC-2 in `docs/MAINNET.md`; the request for proposals is
`docs/AUDIT_SCOPE.md`).

Sections 1–7 and Appendix A retain the historical review record at `6a338b6`; their counts and
open statuses are that baseline, not the final release disposition; 1.6 gives the final status of
every item still open or partial there. Section 8 records completed
round 3; section 9 records the final integration, verification, remaining review limitations and
external release gates, and 9.9 the state on October 18. The working report was not to be
published while F64 (a DOM XSS in the node's built-in explorer) remained live on the public v0.4.0
testnet. On October 18 that testnet was replaced by one whose explorer serves the fix (9.9). This
edition was prepared from the private working report; finding IDs, severities, counts and
statuses are as recorded there. The audit record behind it is kept privately by the maintainer
and is available on request (Appendix A).

**Glossary.**

- **f:** the most faulty validators a set of n tolerates, fewer than n/3. It is counted by validators, not stake: each validator has one vote whatever its stake (DEC-3).
- **Owner key, consensus key:** an account's full-control key (a passkey device or recovery key); a validator's hot signing key.
- **SF-13:** the fix for MAINNET.md SF-13, the rule that lets a validator's consensus key sign system txs (attestations, beacon, evidence, validator updates) with its owner's account as `from`, so the owner pays their fees.
- **Handoff:** the outgoing validator set's signatures passing control to the next set; joiners follow the chain of handoffs from a trusted root.
- **Witness:** an independent node a client asks to confirm the state root behind a proven read.
- **Weak subjectivity:** a new node must trust the genesis and a recent checkpoint, and refuses roots older than the weak-subjectivity period.
- **Séance:** a randomly drawn jury of humans with Soul that compares contribution claims in pairs (commit, then reveal). **Soul:** non-transferable contribution reputation.
- **Duty lane:** mempool room reserved for validators' duty txs. **Tails-only eviction:** the mempool evicts only the last tx of a sender's queue. **Boot share:** a validator's share of the bootstrap delegation (F12).
- **DEC-, SEC-, CRY-, WAL-, TOK-, OPS- ids** refer to `docs/MAINNET.md`.
- **resume-core, RA, resume-relaunch, RI, RP:** Opus 5.5 agents in the final round (section 9). resume-core, RA (the application agent; `resume-apps` in the record) and resume-relaunch wrote follow-up fixes, and RA also verified findings before fixing them. RI reviewed the explorer alignment and RP the final explorer polish; neither wrote code.

### 1.4 The most serious findings and their state

States in 1.3 to 1.5 are at `6a338b6`. Every item this section lists as open went into round 3 (section 8), and 1.6 gives each one's final status.

All eight critical and high findings are fixed at `6a338b6`, and each fix review accepted the fix:

- **F01 (critical):** a tx's tip was an uncapped transfer to the block proposer, paid before any lock or multisig check. One signature could move vesting-locked FANG, a multisig's whole balance, or (through a validator's consensus key) its owner's balance. Fixed in round 1: the tip is capped, paid only from transferable FANG, and refused on txs signed by a multisig owner key or by a consensus key acting for its owner.
- **F02 (high):** consensus signatures didn't bind the chain instance, so a relaunch under the same chain id could turn old certificates into slashing evidence. Round 1 fixed votes and certificates. Round 2 fixed the validator-set handoff signatures that round 1 missed.
- **F04, F06 (high):** a stolen consensus key could drain its owner through tips. Round 1 forbade the tip. Round 2 bounded the base fees such a key can still burn.
- **F05, F07 (high):** fabricated equivocation evidence could slash a pool repeatedly, and the reporter reward made it profitable. Fixed in round 1: a validator is slashed once.
- **F08 (high):** validators signed validator-set handoffs they had never verified, so a malicious fast-sync peer could harvest signatures for made-up transitions. Fixed in round 1. The follow-ups the reviews found were fixed in round 2.
- **xcut-economic-2 (high or medium; the two skeptics disagree):** locked insider allocations could fund one validator per 1% of supply and take more than f seats. Fixed in round 2.

Six medium findings are not fully closed at `6a338b6`. They are in round 3 (final status: 1.6):

- **gap-6-1:** a joiner on a release older than a governance change that shortens the evidence window still accepts roots that are too old.
- **gap-7-1:** with no witnesses configured (the default), the SDK (silently, unless `requireProvenNames` is set) and the web wallet (after a warning, on mainnet too) still sign a `.boo` name's address from one unproven RPC answer. The MCP labels it UNPROVEN and on mainnet asks the user to confirm the address first. The CLI signs the bare name.
- **gap-7-6:** votes and approvals now bind their content on chain, but the clients don't show what they bind: the web wallet and the MCP display the node's own summary (never the kind), and the MCP and the CLI sign a fresh re-read without confirmation. A lying RPC can still get a drain voted for as a harmless proposal. review-r2-clients-3 (medium) is a duplicate of it.
- **xcut-economic-opus-1:** volume can still pay in the contribution pool when a third person takes part in a Séance.
- **review-r2-clients-1:** the round-2 vote binding makes proposals with integer fields above 2^53 impossible to vote on from JavaScript clients.
- **review-r2-sync-1:** round 2's peer-rotation rule lets one peer's fast tiny round bench every honest sync peer.

Two open low items are chain halts (at `6a338b6`; final status: 1.6; severity scale in 2.2):

- **review-r2-gov-1:** once governance has scheduled and then cancelled a `u32::MAX` upgrade, any account's `Propose` of a ScheduleUpgrade panics the node that admits it and, once a proposer includes it, every validator at the same height.
- **review-fix-staking-2:** a newcomer can halt a 2- or 3-member set for good, and only a hard fork recovers.

## 2. Scope and method

### 2.1 Components and lenses

The plan (`fable-audit.js` in the record) split the code into 12 components. Each one got two finder passes:

- **Lens A, correctness and safety:** creating, losing or stealing funds; supply invariants; consensus divergence; authorization bypass; encoding ambiguity; economic rules against `docs/MAINNET-DESIGN.md`.
- **Lens B, adversarial robustness:** panics and arithmetic overflow (release builds run with overflow checks on, so a reachable overflow is a crash), liveness halts, DoS, asymmetric griefing, malicious peers, snapshots and RPCs, unbounded collections, boundary heights, and privacy leaks.

The 12 components were consensus, staking, econ, governance, poc-beacon (Proof of Contribution and the randomness beacon), core (`chain.rs` and `types.rs`), state-sync, ghost, p2p-ops-node, api (with the explorer), clients (SDK, MCP, web wallet) and supply-chain. Two cross-cutting passes followed: xcut-economic (economic and game-theoretic attacks across modules) and xcut-parity (Rust and TypeScript encoding parity). Each finder had to report a concrete exploit path or failure scenario with `file:line`, and was encouraged to write a proof-of-concept test in a scratch worktree. The repository was read-only to every auditor.

The threat model is `docs/AUDIT_SCOPE.md`: fewer than a third of validators Byzantine, any tx sender, network peers and sync sources, anyone on the internet, a malicious RPC, and a supply-chain attacker. A malicious web origin attacking the passkey wallet is also an adversary (WAL-3). It assumes fewer than a third of validators are Byzantine by count (each validator has one vote whatever its stake, DEC-3), at least 2/3 of the set upgrade honestly, GitHub reports merges honestly, and ML-DSA-65 and BLAKE3 are secure.

The early review (2026-10-14) was a separate, smaller Fable run with 8 finder passes: the hybrid p2p handshake, Ghost Mode, state and wire formats, two lenses each.

### 2.2 Verification by skeptics

The rule was two independent skeptics for every finding a finder rated medium or worse, one for low and info, and a third (tiebreak) when two disagreed on whether it was real. A skeptic tries to refute the finding: it reads the cited code and its callers, checks reachability and impact, and writes a PoC when reading can't settle it. A finding counts as confirmed when most of its verdicts call it real.

The rule was not met for the Fable-era findings:

- **F01-F78** each got one Fable group skeptic. One agent verified every finding in a component group. A run from 09:25 on 2026-10-16 wrote 36 verdicts before the usage limit stopped its other groups, and a run from 10:39 wrote the other 42. Second skeptics were launched for F01-F08 (the critical and high ones) in the 09:25 run, but all eight stopped at the usage limit. The 10:39 run re-ran only F08's. So 32 of the 33 findings that finders rated medium or worse rest on one skeptic.
- **supply-chain-B-1** (medium) has one Fable skeptic.
- **Everything verified by Opus 5.5** follows the rule. Medium-or-worse candidates have `o1` and `o2`, and xcut-economic-5 has a tiebreak `o3`. All five xcut-economic findings got two skeptics.
- **The early review's** skeptic stage mostly failed to start. Only 7 skeptic verdicts were completed, covering 3 of the 12 findings (#1, #2, #3). Wave 3's fix agents re-checked each finding before fixing it, and the regression tests for #7, #10 and #11 reproduce the finders' PoCs.

Where two verdicts disagree, this report says so (section 4.3). Where two skeptics give different severities, the tables show both ("high/medium"), and the counts in 1.3 use the higher one.

Severities follow the scale the skeptics were given: **critical**, funds or consensus safety broken by any user; **high**, the same under conditions, or a remote halt; **medium**, bounded loss, DoS, griefing or governance safety; **low**, hard to reach or minor; **info**. A halt that needs governance or a tiny validator set first can therefore be rated low (1.4).

### 2.4 Fix rounds and independent review

Each fix round worked the same way:

1. **Packages.** Items were grouped into packages by code area. Each package ran in its own worktree and branch (`mp/fix-*`, `mp/r2-*`, `mp/r3-*`). A fix agent re-verified each item, fixed it with a regression test, and recorded an outcome line (`fixed`, `already_fixed`, `not_reproducible` or `accepted_risk`) with the tests it added. Before finishing, a fix agent had to run the node's tests, a release build, clippy and the fuzz-crate check (plus the client tests when it touched clients) and record the results.
2. **Integration.** An integrator merged the packages, resolved conflicts, regenerated vectors and fuzz seeds, and ran the full suite: Rust tests in debug and release, clippy, fuzz-crate checks, Ghost tests, SDK vectors and known-answer tests, MCP tests, web typecheck, build and Playwright tests, the SDK end-to-end tests against `blockula dev`, and a 6-node local testnet with a fast-sync join. The reports are `fixes/integration.md` and `fixes/r2-integration.md`.
3. **Fix review.** For every package, Opus 5.5 reviewers worked under two lenses:
   - **R1, does each fix close its root cause?** The reviewer tried variants of the original exploit and checked that the regression test fails without the fix. Verdicts: `accept`, `partial`, `reject` or `not_a_defect_ok`, with a bypass and the remaining work.
   - **R2, what did the diff break?** The reviewer read the package diff as new code and looked for new defects. Each one got skeptics under the rule above.
4. **Integration review.** Two more lenses ran on the integrated branch: I1 checked that no merge lost or weakened a fix, and I2 looked for cross-package interactions none of the packages saw.
5. **Next round.** Every partial fix and every confirmed new finding went into the next round.

Verification did not gate round 1. Fixing began at 09:50 on 2026-10-16 ("Fix confirmed and unverified Fable audit findings"), before 42 of F01-F78 had a skeptic verdict (those were written from 10:44), so for those the fix agent's own re-check came first. Code written for F38, F40, F55 and F78 before they were judged not defects was kept as hardening, and reviewed as such (`not_a_defect_ok`, or partial for F55). For F78 the fix agent recorded the finding as confirmed, against Fable's verdict.

**The origin/main merge.** During round 2, after six of its seven packages had finished, the coordinating Opus 5.5 session merged origin/main at the maintainer's request and resolved its 10 conflicts (`4080056`, 2026-10-17 04:43). It brought PR #11 (QR codes and payment links on Receive) and PR #12 (the animated ASCII backdrop, which the node's explorer now loads under `script-src 'sha256-…' 'strict-dynamic'`). r2-clients finished afterwards, and the round-2 integration was built on the merge. All seven round-2 packages branched from `31d8f5c`, so no round-2 fix agent worked on a tree with PRs #11 and #12; their web edits met those PRs only in the integration. The round-2 review then ran on the integrated `6a338b6`.

Opus 5.5 reviewed the merge before it was committed (04:29-04:42) with two lenses: lost (did the resolution drop either side's changes?) and semantic (do the two sides' changes interact?). It checked the conflict resolution, not the 2,860 lines of PRs #11 and #12, which never had a component pass. It raised 5 candidates, and one skeptic each confirmed 4 of them (all low):

### 2.5 Limits of the method

- **Every reviewer was an AI model.** Two models were used, and from 2026-10-16 18:31 only one, until a Codex session joined the final round on October 17 at 21:25 ET. Both are Anthropic Claude models, so their blind spots may be correlated. Opus 5.5 wrote the fixes and also reviewed them, so fix review was independent at the level of agents, not models. The same model also wrote Fable's scopes and prompts, as the coordinator. The Codex session integrated, inspected and tested the final round's work and wrote some fixes itself (section 9); its checks of its own commits are author checks (9.6).
- **The design was taken as given.** Finders were told to report documented limits only where the code is worse than documented, and skeptics treated documented trade-offs as not defects (xcut-economic-5 was rejected on exactly that ground). Whether the design's choices are sound (the council's powers, one vote per validator, weak subjectivity, the insider allocations) was out of scope.
- **Fix agents verified their own work** before the independent review. The reviewers did check the tests. In round 1 they confirmed that the regression test fails without the fix for 72 of 77 items (2 not checked, 2 without a test, 1 "no": F57). In round 2 they confirmed it for 61 of 62 (1 "no": supply-chain-B-4).
- **Fewer skeptics than planned** for most Fable-era findings (section 2.2).
- **Fix code got no full re-audit.** It was reviewed through the R1, R2, I1 and I2 lenses only. The 23 component passes ran at `1f3af2b`. The polish merge in `e9bbc08` got only supply-chain-B, the cross-cutting passes and the gap round, whose four polish-merge passes found nothing. From `e9bbc08` to `6a338b6`, `node/src` went from 38,136 to 49,503 lines of Rust (+12,711/−1,324, tests included), so about a quarter of the node code at `6a338b6` was written after the last component pass and was read only as fix diffs. The SDK, MCP and web wallet gained 4,469 lines, about 2,800 of them PR #11's and #12's web code, which got only the merge review (2.4). xcut-parity ran at `e9bbc08`, before round 2 changed the signed encodings of AddKey, Vote, MultisigApprove and CommitVotes; their parity rests on the regenerated vectors and the R2 review, and review-r2-clients-1 (an open JS encoder failure) shows that this area can still break.
- **Nothing was tested on live infrastructure.** Agents were told never to push or touch live infrastructure, and from round 2 on never to touch GitHub settings. The exceptions on record: skeptics read the repository's visibility and rulesets with read-only `gh api` calls (supply-chain-B-1, supply-chain-B-3), and the round-2 reviewer read the live ruleset (supply-chain-B-3). The GitHub settings changes in section 6.2 were made by the maintainer's own session, not by audit agents. Until the October 18 relaunch, the record shows no agent testing against the public testnet or the Fly deployment. After it, the integration owner checked the relaunched public testnet, including F64's fix on its explorer (9.9).
- **Not covered:**
  - the internals of Commonware `=2026.9.0` (simplex and marshal are BETA; the Application trait and `marshal::application` are ALPHA, per AUDIT_SCOPE.md), `ml-dsa`, `@noble/post-quantum`, Plonky3, and the pinned storage and encoding crates (`redb`, `borsh`, `imbl`);
  - constant-time behaviour and side channels in the signature libraries (AUDIT_SCOPE question 6);
  - the passkey flow on real authenticators and platforms (CI covers it only with a virtual PRF authenticator, MAINNET.md WAL-1; PLAN.md's line that `sdk-ts/src/passkey.ts` "hasn't been exercised in a real browser yet" is out of date);
  - the web wallet's hosting and origin (the Vercel project and domain; WAL-3, DEC-8);
  - the genesis ceremony; legal questions.
- **The process was interrupted several times** by usage and session limits (section 3). Each restart resumed from persisted results and checkpoint commits. The record shows no lost item, but restarts are where gaps hide.

Locks were checked only against the action phase, so the whole tip could come from vesting- or vote-locked FANG. Multisig proposals and approvals skipped the threshold check at admission, so one owner key could tip away the account. A validator's consensus key, signing system txs for its owner (SF-13), could tip the owner's balance to itself as proposer.
- **Fix:** round 1, `mp/fix-fees` `a8e4b2e` (merged as `bdfcf72`). The tip is capped at `tip_cap_multiple` x the base fee (new parameter, 20 at genesis, bounds 1-1,000), it must come from transferable FANG, and it is refused on txs signed by a multisig owner key or by a consensus key acting for its owner. State schema 6. The integrator made `tip_cap_multiple` changeable only by a token supermajority (`d79a02e`), since the bootstrap council is the validators.
- **Verification:** R1 review accept; the tests fail without the fix.
- **Residual:** none recorded for F01. Related: review-fix-fees-2 and review-integrate-merges-1, both fixed in round 2.

#### F02 (high): consensus signatures didn't bind the chain instance

- **Found and verified:** Fable, consensus-A (`consensus.rs:76`). One Fable skeptic: high.
- **What and impact:** the only domain separator for votes and certificates was `blockula/{chain_id}`. A relaunch that keeps the chain id and validator keys (as testnet relaunches do) shares valid signatures with the old instance. Old finalization certificates could then be presented as equivocation evidence that slashes and tombstones honest validators.
- **Fix:** round 1, `mp/fix-staking` `b4b7a7c` (merged as `8d2f3f9`). The namespace is now `blockula/<chain>/<genesis hash>` for every vote, certificate, evidence check and manifest check. `STORAGE_VERSION` became `prunable-v7`. The R1 review judged it **partial**: validator-set handoff signatures, made with the same keys, still bound only the chain id (reported again as review-integrate-compose-1). Round 2, `mp/r2-sync` `c2d6506` (merged as `37e74f9`): handoffs sign `BLOCKULA_HANDOFF_V3` with the genesis hash (`HANDOFF_FORMAT` 3).
- **Verification:** R1 review partial, R2 review accept; the tests fail without the fix.
- **Residual:** optional hardening only. The reviewer suggests binding the genesis hash into the validator proof of possession too.

#### F04 (high): a stolen consensus key could drain its owner through tips

- **Found and verified:** Fable, governance-B (`chain.rs:1140`). One Fable skeptic: high, with two PoCs.
- **What and impact:** under SF-13 a validator's consensus key may sign certain system txs with `from` = its owner. Those skipped the multisig check, and their owner-paid tip had no bound. A stolen hot key could move a multisig's or a vesting-locked owner's balance to the proposer.
- **Fix:** round 1, `mp/fix-fees`: such txs must carry tip 0. The R1 review judged it **partial**. A stolen key could still burn a fully locked owner's FANG through base fees (in the probe, 64 no-op `UpdateValidator`s a block, about 0.0041 FANG a block at the floor fee), and the keys of tombstoned and removed validators kept their standing. Round 2, `mp/r2-staking` `fcc4030` (merged as `e81acd7`):
  - an SF-13 tx whose action fails is refused, not charged;
  - an `UpdateValidator` from the consensus key for its owner applies at most once an epoch;
  - tombstoned and removed validators' keys are refused except for a `BeaconReveal`.
- **Verification:** R1 review partial, R2 review accept; the tests fail without the fix.
- **Residual (PLAN.md "Known limits"):** a stolen key can still make its owner pay a few base fees an epoch and take owner nonces. Protocol v1 has no owner-signed rotation or revocation of a consensus key. Related: review-r2-integrate-compose-1 (round 3).

#### F06 (high): uncapped owner-paid tip on consensus-key system txs

- **What and impact:** the same root cause as F04, seen from staking. A compromised hot key could tip its cold owner's liquid balance to the validator address it controls.

#### F05 and F07 (high): fabricated equivocation evidence and unbounded repeated slashing

- **Found and verified:** Fable, staking-A (F05, `staking.rs:687`) and staking-B (F07, `staking.rs:681`). One Fable skeptic each: high. The F07 skeptic calls it "the same defect as F05, confirmed independently".
- **What and impact:** reports were deduplicated on (validator, epoch, view), and `verify_evidence` accepted any view. Anyone holding a consensus key (a validator, or a thief) could sign conflicting votes at arbitrary views and file one 5% slash after another against the pool, mostly delegators' stake. The 10% reporter reward made a sock-puppet report profitable.
- **Fix:** round 1, `mp/fix-staking` `b4b7a7c`. A double-signer is slashed once: the pool records `slashed_from` and is tombstoned. Evidence views are bounded to `VIEW_SLACK` (1,000) past the epoch's highest executed view (a new table, schema 6). The reporter reward is capped at what the validator's owner lost.
- **Verification:** R1 review accept for both; the tests fail without the fix. A property test and a simulator scenario cover conservation and identical slashing on every node.
- **Residual:** none recorded. Equivocation is still caught only if some honest node sees both votes (PLAN.md).

#### F08 (high): validators signed handoffs they never verified

- **Found and verified:** Fable, state-sync-A (`epochs.rs:99`). Two Fable skeptics (`g`, `s`): both high, and `s` with a passing PoC.
- **What and impact:** `verify_manifest` verified validator-set changes only through the manifest's last certified epoch, then returned the peer's whole registry, which `install_snapshot` adopted. `sign_missing` then signed a handoff for every recorded change, and the handoff message bound neither the outgoing set nor the signer. A malicious fast-sync peer could plant made-up transitions and harvest real validators' signatures for them (a GET on `/api/epochs`). Those signatures are the raw material of a forged validator-set history.
- **Fix:** round 1, `mp/fix-sync` `e9c3355` (merged as `07fbf76`):
  - a validator signs a change only when its own executed (or certified) state holds both sets;
  - `BLOCKULA_HANDOFF_V2` binds the outgoing set's hash;
  - a fast-synced node adopts only the verified history;
  - a genesis home ignores a saved `epochs.json`, and history past the executed epoch is dropped on open.
- **Verification:** R1 review accept; the tests fail without the fix. The R2 lens and I2 found follow-ups (review-fix-sync-2, -4, -5 and review-integrate-compose-1), all fixed in round 2.
- **Residual:** sync stays weakly subjective (PLAN.md). Joiners must pin the genesis and recent checkpoints.

#### xcut-economic-2 (high/medium): insider seats were bounded per pool only

- **Found and verified:** Fable, xcut-economic at `e9bbc08` (`staking.rs:545`). Opus skeptics: **o1 high, o2 medium.**
- **What and impact:** the insider delegation cap bounded one pool at 1% of supply, and locked tokens could fund self-bonds. A vesting allocation could register one validator per 1% of supply. The finder estimates the founder and foundation alone could take 15 of 31 seats (more than f) in two epochs.
- **Fix:** round 2, `mp/r2-staking` `fcc4030`:
  - in a pool's ranking stake, stake from still-vesting accounts other than the owner doesn't count;
  - an account with unvested tokens joins as a newcomer only if it holds no other seat;
  - the founder's and foundation's accounts (`bootstrap_excluded`) hold at most min(2, f-1) seats together.
- **Verification:** R2 review accept; the test fails without the fix.
- **Residual (PLAN.md):** an insider's vested (liquid) tokens back validators like anyone's, and seats held before a rule binds them (genesis seats) are not taken away. The reviewer suggests, optionally, applying the seat cap to kept seats when the set shrinks.

#### F03 (medium): vote and vesting locks overlapped, enabling double votes

- **Found and verified:** Fable, governance-A (`econ.rs:386`), rated high by the finder. One Fable skeptic: medium, with a PoC.
- **What and impact:** locks combined by maximum, but votes use only vested tokens. A vesting account's vote of X locked nothing new while X was at most its unvested amount, so it could move the voted tokens to another account and vote again. A merged duplicate added that unrealized staking gains covered the vesting lock.
- **Fix:** round 1, `mp/fix-gov` `4709603` (merged as `4660409`): vote locks stack on vesting locks, and a vesting account's stake counts at cost. Round 2 (as xcut-economic-1, `mp/r2-staking`): the voting balance also counts stake at cost while tokens are unvested, so unrealized gains don't vote.
- **Verification:** R1 review accept (for F03 itself); R2 review accept for the remainder.
- **Residual:** none. Unrealized gains count as unvested until the lock fully ends, which the reviewer calls conservative.

#### F09 (medium): `/api/sync` gaps and an unbudgeted response

#### F10 (medium): the validator-set registry grew without bound

- **Found and verified:** Fable, api-A (`api.rs:1175`). One Fable skeptic: medium.
- **What and impact:** every set change added about 330 KB of keys and signatures, and every manifest and `/api/epochs` response carried the whole registry. Joiners cap those responses, so handoff exchange and fast sync would stop working after roughly 50 to 200 set changes.

#### F12 (medium): mainnet genesis stranded the bootstrap-delegation bucket

- **Found and verified:** Fable, econ-B (`genesis_builder.rs:55`). One Fable skeptic: medium, after rerunning the PoC.

#### F14 (medium): one multisig owner key could freeze the account

#### F15 (medium): governance safety parameters changed at the weakest threshold

- **Found and verified:** Fable, governance-A (`governance.rs:560`). One Fable skeptic: medium.
- **What and impact:** only a few proposal kinds needed the supermajority. The bootstrap council could extend itself to 10 years and rewrite quorum, timelock and voting period. A simple majority could lower the supermajority to 50.01% and the timelock to one block before scheduling an upgrade.
- **Fix:** round 1, `mp/fix-gov`. The safety parameters (`GOVERNANCE_KEYS`) need the token supermajority, never pass by the council, and keep hard floors, checked at propose and again at execution. The reviewer noted that the floors had no ceilings, so a single vote could make later votes impossible (review-fix-gov-3). Round 2 added ceilings: GOVERNANCE.md lists quorum at 1-10% of unlocked supply and 5M-50M FANG, and the supermajority at 60-80%.
- **Verification:** R1 review accept; review-fix-gov-3 accepted in round 2.
- **Residual:**
  - `proposal_deposit` and `multisig_expiry_blocks` remain council keys (`params::COUNCIL_KEYS`; GOVERNANCE.md lists both): the bootstrap council can raise the deposit to 1M FANG, pricing ordinary holders out of proposing, and cut every multisig's approval window to 10 blocks (R1 review hardening note; neither round 2 nor round 3 took it up).
  - The 50M FANG `quorum_floor` ceiling isn't tied to the votable supply, so early on, while less than 50M FANG can vote, one token supermajority can set a quorum no later vote can reach, including the one that would undo it (R2 review of review-fix-gov-3, optional). That qualifies GOVERNANCE.md's "whatever a vote sets, the next vote can set back".

#### F18 (medium): slashing protection's restart escape fired on silence

- **Found and verified:** Fable, p2p-ops-node-A (`protection.rs:339`). One Fable skeptic: medium, with a PoC.
- **What and impact:** after about 20 s without certificates for its own epoch, a restarting validator was allowed to sign on its record alone. A validator restored from an old volume while the network was in a later epoch, or simply invisible to it, would then re-sign views its earlier run had voted in, producing slashable double-sign evidence.
- **Fix:** round 1, `mp/fix-staking`. The escape also needs HTTP peers to show the network standing still at this node's height (or `--network-halted-at` while no peer answers) and no sign of a later epoch. The R1 review judged it **partial**: a liveness regression, since one unverified source could veto a whole-network restart (also review-fix-staking-1 and -3). Round 2, `mp/r2-consensus` `3e9f5f3` (merged as `aa9a119`):
  - a later epoch counts only from verified artifacts or from more than f distinct members of the epoch's set, and such hints expire;
  - peers at this height must outnumber peers claiming more;
  - a peer that claims to be ahead must prove it with certified blocks;
  - `--network-halted-at` overrides unverified answers.
- **Verification:** R1 review partial, R2 review accept; the tests fail without the fix. Simulator scenarios include a restored validator cut off from the network, and a whole-network restart with one lying validator.
- **Residual (PLAN.md):** `/api/status` answers are unsigned. A restored validator can still escape under an eclipse: peers at its height outnumber the honest ones ahead, and those can't prove it. The reviewer adds that restoring the validator together with its sentries from one old backup produces exactly that case. Never restore a validator volume (OPERATIONS.md). The operator flag also overrides unverified answers, including honest peers that can't prove they're ahead because they pruned (in F18's exact case, a restore older than the peers' ~2-day window, their `/api/sync` answers 410). On a validator restored from an old volume, a mistaken `--network-halted-at` reopens the double-sign. The reviewer suggests naming 410 or pruned answers in the abstain warning and retrying failed probes.

#### F19 (medium): handoff-proof exchange was unbounded and ran under the chain lock

- **Verification:** R2 review **partial**. With one more account in the Séance, the farmer still beats the solid contributor, because the per-person square root over k claims beats one claim whenever k x score > 1, and Soul isn't damped per person.
- **Residual:** in round 3 (r3-poc; final status: 1.6).

#### xcut-economic-opus-2 (medium): a sybil's voucher escaped the penalty

- **Found and verified:** Opus 5.5, economic continuation (`poc.rs:732`). o1 and o2 medium, with independent PoCs.
- **What and impact:** the vouchee could drop the vouch for free while a FlagSybil proposal sat in its 9-day vote and timelock. The flag then found no vouch, and the voucher kept its bond and 50 Soul.
- **Fix:** round 2, `mp/r2-poc`. A dropped vouch is kept as a record (state namespace 26) that holds the bond and the slot until the vouch's lock ends and while any FlagSybil proposal names the vouchee. The flag slashes the held bond, takes the Soul and claws back the voucher's cuts. State schema 7.
- **Verification:** R2 review accept; both tests fail without the fix.
- **Residual:** liability ends with the vouch lock (about 90 days). Any FlagSybil proposal can hold a withdrawn voucher's 10 FANG while it is open, which costs the proposer a deposit.

#### gap-6-1 (medium/low): the weak-subjectivity period ignored governance

- **Found and verified:** Opus 5.5, gap-6 at `e9bbc08` (`checkpoints.rs:180`). **o1 medium, o2 low.**
- **What and impact:** nodes computed the period from the genesis parameters only. After governance shortened the evidence window, joiners and restarting nodes still accepted roots up to 10.5 days old, signed by keys no longer slashable.
- **Fix:** round 2, `mp/r2-sync`. A node enforces the shortest of the genesis period, the release's recorded `weak_subjectivity_blocks` and its own executed state's period. Round 2's governance package also made `evidence_max_age_epochs` a token-supermajority parameter.
- **Verification:** R2 review **partial**. A joiner on a release older than the shortening has no state to follow and still accepts the old period.
- **Residual:** in round 3 (r3-sync; final status: 1.6). PLAN.md documents it. The reviewer's proposed fix is to read peers' `weak_subjectivity_blocks` at join, or to set a protocol floor on the evidence window.

#### gap-7-1 (medium): clients signed name resolutions from one unproven RPC read

- **Found and verified:** Opus 5.5, gap-7 at `e9bbc08` (`sdk-ts/src/index.ts:965`). o1 and o2 medium, with the finder's PoC rerun.
- **What and impact:** the SDK, MCP, web wallet and CLI turned a `.boo` name into an address with one unproven `/api/name` read and signed the address. A lying RPC could redirect payments, hunt awards, vouches, name transfers, treasury-spend targets and stake.
- **Fix:** round 2, `mp/r2-clients` `84a1570` (merged as `0b845f5`):
  - SDK: `Client` takes witnesses and proves names with `provenName`;
  - MCP: `BLOCKULA_WITNESSES`, "UNPROVEN" labels, and on mainnet no signature until the user confirms the address;
  - web: `NEXT_PUBLIC_*_WITNESSES`;
  - Rust CLI: signs the bare name wherever the chain resolves it.
- **Verification:** R2 review **partial**. It is closed where witnesses are configured, but witnesses are off by default, and the SDK and web wallet then still sign the node's answer.
- **Residual:** in round 3 (r3-clients; final status: 1.6). Configure witnesses on mainnet (section 6.2).

#### gap-7-2 (medium): AddKey had no proof of possession

- **Found and verified:** Opus 5.5, gap-7 (`api.rs:1054`). o1 and o2 medium, with the PoC rerun.
- **What and impact:** any account could add any key id as an owner key, and `/api/key` listed only the first 100 holders in address order. About 11 FANG of sybil accounts could hide a victim's account from recovery-kit, linked-passkey and MCP `use_account` flows and offer the attacker's accounts instead.
- **Fix:** round 2, `mp/r2-clients`. AddKey (tag 2) carries the key's public key and an ML-DSA-65 proof of possession over the chain id, genesis hash, account and key id (context `blockula/key-pop/v1`), verified at execution. `/api/key` is paged. Rust and TS encodings match.
- **Verification:** R2 review accept; the tests fail without the fix.
- **Residual (PLAN.md):** consent doesn't expire. The reviewer notes a lying-RPC path on web `/recover` (the WAL-4 class).

#### gap-7-6 (medium): approvals and ballots didn't bind their content

- **Found and verified:** Opus 5.5, gap-7 (`governance.rs:993`). o1 and o2 medium.
- **What and impact:** `MultisigApprove{id}`, `Vote{proposal_id}` and Séance a/b letters named only an id or position that the signer learned from its RPC. A lying RPC could decide what its users approved or voted on.
- **Fix:** round 2, `mp/r2-clients`. `MultisigApprove` carries `action_digest`, `Vote` carries `proposal_digest` and `CommitVotes` carries `pairs_digest`, all checked at execution (tags 44, 40 and 25).

On chain the binding is sound, but the clients don't show what they bind: the web wallet and the MCP display the node's own summary (never the kind), and the MCP and the CLI bind a fresh re-read without confirmation. The reviewer reproduced it with the built MCP: a 10M FANG treasury spend shown as "plant garlic (no funds move)" was voted aye, and the chain accepted the vote. The round-2 R2 lens found the related review-r2-clients-2 and -3 (duplicates) and review-r2-clients-1 (a new defect from the binding).
- **Residual:** in round 3 (r3-clients; the related review-r2-clients-1 is in r3-gov; final status: 1.6). PLAN.md: the binding covers the kind, title, link, claim ids, repos and PR numbers, not what the linked pages say. #### review-fix-sync-1 (medium): `fetch_history` had no deadline

- **Found and verified:** Opus 5.5, round-1 R2 review of `mp/fix-sync` (`snapshot.rs:645`). o1 and o2 medium.

#### review-r2-clients-1 (medium, open): some proposals can't be voted on from JS clients

#### review-r2-sync-1 (medium, open): one fast tiny round benches every honest sync peer

- **Found and verified:** Opus 5.5, round-2 R2 review of `mp/r2-sync` at `6a338b6` (`node.rs:1153`). o1 and o2 medium, with deterministic simulations.

## 6. Accepted risks and known limits after the audit

### 6.1 Residual risk by finding

These remain at `6a338b6` after the fixes; the items marked round 3 were taken up later, and 1.6 gives their final status. Most were written into `docs/PLAN.md` "Known limits" (or `docs/MAINNET.md`) by the fix agents themselves during rounds 1 and 2. Being documented means they are disclosed, not that anyone independent has accepted them.

- **F35, name front-running (accepted risk).** Mempools admit only the first account's registration of a name while that one can still be included, for at most 17 blocks after it arrived, and multisig-wrapped registrations count too (round 2). A proposer can still put its own registration first in a block it proposes, someone who reaches a node before the first asker's tx can win there, and a k-of-n multisig's registration holds the name only from its completing approval. Commit-reveal registration is not built. Send a registration that matters to a trusted node, with a tip.
- **F04 and F06, SF-13.** A stolen consensus key can't move FANG, but it can make its owner pay a few base fees each epoch (one UpdateValidator, a beacon commitment and reveal, attestations, real fault reports) and take owner nonces. There is no owner-signed rotation or revocation of a consensus key, and no on-chain opt-out. review-r2-integrate-compose-1 (round 3; at `6a338b6`; final status: 1.6) is a related bypass of a multisig owner.
- **F18, slashing protection.** The restart escape relies on unsigned HTTP answers. An eclipse can still let a restored validator escape, and so can a mistaken `--network-halted-at` on a validator restored from an old volume. Never restore a validator volume.
- **review-fix-staking-2, tiny sets (round 3; at `6a338b6`; final status: 1.6).** A newcomer into a set of 2 or 3 that shows up and then goes silent halts the chain for good, and only a hard fork recovers (PLAN.md). Round 3 is working on it. Whether the remainder is accepted is still to be decided.
- **gap-6-1, weak subjectivity (round 3; at `6a338b6`; final status: 1.6).** A joiner on a release older than a governance shortening of the evidence window still accepts the old period.
- **gap-7-1, witnesses off by default (round 3; at `6a338b6`; final status: 1.6).** The SDK and MCP use no witnesses by default, and the web wallet uses none unless `NEXT_PUBLIC_*_WITNESSES` is set. Without witnesses, name resolution is one node's word. The SDK signs the node's answer silently unless the caller sets `requireProvenNames`, and the web wallet warns and then signs, on mainnet too. The MCP labels it UNPROVEN and on mainnet asks the user to confirm the address first. The CLI signs the bare name, which the chain resolves.
- **gap-7-6, what signers bind (round 3; an open defect at `6a338b6`; final status: 1.6).** On chain, votes, approvals and Séance judgments bind the proposal's kind, title and URL, the stored action, and the pairs. But the shipped clients don't bind what they show: the web wallet and the MCP display the node's unbound summary, and the MCP and the CLI sign a fresh re-read without asking. So a lying RPC can still get a drain voted for as a harmless proposal. The wrong page behind a URL is out of reach of any binding.
- **F67, proven reads.** Witnesses are trusted for the state root, and a proof may lag by up to `maxLagBlocks`. Certified roots (WAL-4) would remove that trust.
- **gap-7-3 (fixed) and gap-7-5 (partial, round 3; at `6a338b6`; final status: 1.6), node-reported values.** Expiries and `propose`'s default deposit start from one node's report, within stated bounds. A partial unstake's share conversion is not effectively bounded without witnesses (the default, on mainnet too): a node that reports the stake as worth the amount plus one drop turns a small unstake into nearly all the shares (MCP and web; against the built MCP, the reviewer reproduced "unstake 10 FANG" of a 1,000 FANG stake signing about 99.99% of the shares). No FANG leaves the account, but the stake stops earning and is locked for the unbonding period. The reviewer says PLAN.md's "bounded" overstates it.
- **gap-7-2, key consent.** AddKey's proof of possession doesn't expire and isn't tied to a nonce.
- **F68, F72 and F74, MCP caps.** The caps are client-side and price names from the pinned node. Only a session key's limit is enforced by the chain.
- **F32 and F56, duty lane.** The lane is local mempool policy. Duties queued behind other txs from the paying account are ordinary txs.
- **F21, beacon.** A validator that sees everyone else's reveals can withhold its own, choosing between two values, at the cost of a slash and two epochs' rewards.
- **F49, installs (round 3; at `6a338b6`; final status: 1.6).** The one-line install trusts the script it downloads, and a first install has no verifier. The release key id isn't generated yet (SECURITY.md).
- **supply-chain-B-1, fuzz.** A red nightly job still tells everyone which target crashed. If `FUZZ_CRASH_AGE_RECIPIENTS` is unset, crashes aren't kept.
- **supply-chain-B-3, GitHub settings (round 3; at `6a338b6`; final status: 1.6).** The rulesets live in `deploy/github/`, but only the admin can apply them and nothing detects drift. With one maintainer, every PR merges through the admin bypass. (October 18: PRs #9, #13 and #18 were merged that way. Later PRs are opened and pushed by a GitHub App the maintainer owns, so that the code owner can approve them; #19 and #20 were approved and merged without a bypass, 9.9.)
- **xcut-economic-2, insider seats.** Seats are bounded by account. Vested insider tokens and genesis seats are not limited further.
- **F12 and review-fix-gov-1, bootstrap delegation.** An operator gets one boot share per seat, DEC-14's hosting caps aren't on-chain for later validators, and stake the delegation moved out of a pool isn't reached by evidence for an earlier epoch.
- **The bootstrap council** (F15, F16, review-integrate-merges-1, xcut-economic-5). Until `bootstrap_end_height` (a year), a proposal also passes if two thirds of the active validators vote aye and token-weighted nays don't exceed the ayes. The council can pass upgrades, their cancellations, feature switches, every other proposal kind, and changes to `params::COUNCIL_KEYS`. It cannot change governance's safety parameters or the validators' own economics (`GOVERNANCE_KEYS`, `VALIDATOR_KEYS`), which are the token holders' alone (GOVERNANCE.md). xcut-economic-5 (judged not a defect, 2 of 3) notes that a liquid holder can veto a council proposal at conviction 6 while the council's own votable FANG is small. The reviews note what the council keys still allow: shrinking `max_validators`, which evicts the lowest-stake validators and moves their share of issuance and commission to the rest (R2 review of review-integrate-merges-1; a raise-only rule would match the design's principle); and raising `proposal_deposit` to 1M FANG and cutting `multisig_expiry_blocks` to 10 blocks (R1 review of F15).
- **F62 and review-fix-sync-1, fast sync.** A malicious peer can hold one attempt for up to about 2.8 hours.
- **Early #12, fast sync.** A joiner rebuilds a peer-chosen state of up to 4 GiB of records before it can check the root.
- **Early #2 and #4.** The empty ML-DSA context shared by the handshake and txs, and the stack copies of ML-KEM secrets, are notes for the external audit (SEC-1).
- **Proof of Contribution.** Validators are trusted to report GitHub honestly. Jury collusion is possible. Reverts are found only in specific shapes within about 30 days. Hunt awards are trusted to the creator.
- **Wallet.** The passkey flow is tested in CI only with a virtual PRF authenticator (MAINNET.md WAL-1); the record has no testing on real authenticators and platforms. Whoever controls the web wallet's origin (today `blockula-wallet.vercel.app`) controls its users' keys (WAL-3, DEC-8), and this review didn't look at the hosting.

### 9.5 Residuals after the final round

None of these was judged launch-blocking by the reviewer that recorded it. "Documented" means
the repository's docs say so.

- **Explorer forecasts leave out queued creation fees (advisory; the chain enforces the
  rules).** The preflight reserves what the sender's pending txs will take (`txDebit`) without a
  first payment's creation fee or a renewal's price, and counts an agent key's pending payments
  against its budget with no creation fee (`web/lib/explorer/preflight.ts:259`; RI-2, info, and
  residual (c) of render-2/protocol-2's R1 review). So a pending payment that opens an
  account is under-counted, and one that opens the same recipient makes the new payment's
  creation fee over-counted. The forecast can be overridden ("Sign & send anyway"); the chain
  checks the session limit, the balance and the fees itself. The node doesn't report per-tx
  creation burns, so a ledger row that may have opened an account says "exact: no", with a
  note, rather than a number.
- **Index rows written by older binaries (low; moot for the relaunch).** `54e1d97` changes what
  new index rows record, not rows already written. On a chain upgraded in place, txs indexed by
  an older binary keep their end-of-block resolutions, so `/api/tx`'s `to` for them can be wrong
  when a name changed hands or lapsed later in the same block. The relaunched testnet starts
  from a new genesis and has no such rows. RP further notes that the sender fallback can
  misattribute such a legacy lapsed-name row to the sender; reindex in-place upgrades rather
  than reuse those old rows. A pre-existing multisig-approved transfer also omits its payee
  from account-history rows (it is not misattributed).
- **A substantive file with no diff can veto the exact-blob revert rule (low; documented in
  PLAN.md "Reverts").** A file too large for GitHub to diff can't be given back once a later PR
  changes it again. So a claimant whose PR has few such files can add one (a dependency, so the
  PR changes a large lockfile) and keep a real revert from being attested. Only `reject_claim`
  catches that revert: an admin tx on test chains, a `RejectClaim` governance proposal on
  mainnet.
- **Release-ceremony fixtures (low; documented in OPERATIONS.md "The release key").** `635c192`
  makes the `release_cli` unit tests pass whatever key id is built in (RA checked them with test
  ids built in). But `scripts/test-release-runbook.sh`'s first-install cases sign with generated
  keys, so once the ceremony builds in the real key id they are refused and the required Node
  check fails. The ceremony PR needs a build with a committed test key first, never a bypass or
  deleted cases. `scripts/test-install.sh` still stubs the verifier. The release key itself
  isn't generated yet (6.2).
- **`tx_status` beyond the nonce (low; documented in PLAN.md).** With witnesses, the MCP proves
  only the account's nonce. Which tx used it (a sibling's status, the node's 404 and its
  `history_from`) is still the node's word and isn't asked of a witness, so `nonce_used`'s "this
  one most likely never ran" can be a lie; the answer now says so. The optional single-view
  nonce read suggested in review-r3-wallet-1 wasn't done.
- **The rolling-restart gate (low; the fresh integration reviewer's limits).** `fad7bf8` proves
  that a restarted node is back on the pinned genesis and has finalized blocks past its
  pre-restart height plus 6, not that it signs: a syncing node passes. It relies on
  `fly ssh console -C` passing the remote exit status through and on `fly secrets unset` waiting
  for the restart, and neither can be checked offline.
- **Relaunch operations (noted by resume-relaunch, not changed).** A machine Fly replaces on the
  same volume won't start until its `machine.lock` is removed, deliberately with no entrypoint
  switch. Rebuilding the public node reruns all of `launch.sh`, redeploying every validator. A
  long fast sync can outlast `fly deploy`'s health wait (rerun it).
- **Genesis clocks (documented in PLAN.md).** The MCP and the web wallet take a chain's genesis
  clock from its node on first use unless a release pins it (`832eedd`; testnet's is pinned
  after launch by `pin-network.sh`). The CLI checks it. Mainnet's clock pin isn't in
  GENESIS_CEREMONY.md's steps yet, a follow-up for the ceremony.
- **Smaller leftovers (info or cosmetic).** The name page still shows Accept and Withdraw on a
  name in grace (RI-1); the preview now refuses both (`0d3d8ee`, `252278c`), as the chain does.
  The hunt page offers Cancel one block later than the chain allows. The SDK's
  `Client.resolution` refuses any input that starts with `boo1` or `tboo1`, so a registered name
  such as `boo1shop.boo` can't be paid by name; this predates main13, and RI noted it without
  filing it. RP P5 also records that the field check accepts short address-like inputs as names
  before that SDK refusal; no truncated address can thereby be signed to a squatted name.
  Without witnesses a name resolution can still skew a forecast, not what is signed; an
  Offer form that forecasts accept/withdraw has a truthful headline but no wrong-form refusal.
  A pending nonce-0 claim has no creation-fee note. Metadata tests omit some lapse/replay
  variants, and Alerts' 25-entry push-out case lacks a test (RP's coverage limits).
  review-r3-gov-3's mirror case (a timelock raised mid-vote) stays a documented known
  limit, and supply-chain-B-3's optional hardening (refusing `checks: write` in workflows)
  wasn't done.

### A.2 Recomputing the counts

In the record, `gen-summary.py` regenerates `verdict-summary.md`, which lists every finding with its verdicts and fix outcomes by round. The counts in this report come from a script over the same files, with these rules:

- **Confirmed:** most of a finding's verdicts say real.
- **Duplicate:** most of the real verdicts name a `duplicate_of`. F50 is the exception: it is kept because the finding it duplicates, F48, was judged not a defect.
- **Severity:** the highest severity among the real verdicts.
- **Status:** each round's fix outcome together with that round's review verdict. A merged duplicate's state counts toward the finding it duplicates, so F36 is open (its duplicate review-r2-consensus-1 reopened it).
