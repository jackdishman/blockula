# Governance, treasury and multisig accounts

Protocol v1 has no admin keys on mainnet (DEC-10). Everything an admin used to decide is a proposal that FANG holders vote on, with a timelock before it runs; during the first year the validators can also pass proposals as a bootstrap council. This page specifies proposals and votes (GOV-1), the treasury and community distributions (TOK-3), multisig accounts (SF-11), and what replaced the Season 0 admin levers (GOV-2, GOV-3). The code is `node/src/governance.rs`; MAINNET-DESIGN 3.5 is the summary.

Every number below is a governance parameter (`node/src/params.rs`), shown with its mainnet default.

| Parameter | Default | |
|---|---|---|
| `proposal_deposit` | 1,000 FANG | least a proposal bonds |
| `voting_blocks` | 7 days | votes count from the proposal's block through `created_at + voting_blocks` |
| `timelock_blocks` | 2 days | a passed proposal runs this long after voting ends |
| `quorum_bps`, `quorum_floor` | 4%, 20M FANG | quorum is the larger of the two |
| `supermajority_bps` | 66.67% | for upgrades and their cancellations, feature activations, the parameters that are the token holders' alone (governance's safety parameters and the validators' own economics), large treasury spends |
| `treasury_large_bps` | 1% | a spend above this share of the treasury is large |
| `lock_period_blocks` | 7 days | one conviction lock period |
| `governance_spend_height` | ≈ 6 months | treasury spends and distributions are refused before it |
| `bootstrap_end_height` | 1 year | the council exists until then |
| `multisig_expiry_blocks` | 7 days | how long a multisig proposal waits for approvals |

`blockula dev` shortens the periods to minutes and opens spending at once (`ChainParams::dev_overrides`): with its 1 s blocks and 60-block epochs, votes last 60 blocks, the timelock and the lock period 20, unbonding 120 and jail 60, and the deposit is 10 FANG. A dev chain's single validator is the whole bootstrap council, so its key passes a proposal alone (the SDK e2e does).

## Proposals

`Propose { kind, deposit, title, url }` (owner keys only) escrows `deposit` (at least `proposal_deposit`) and opens a vote. What can be known to fail is refused right away: a parameter outside its bounds, a spend before `governance_spend_height`, an unknown feature, a hunt that isn't open, a claim or GitHub link that doesn't exist, an upgrade or claim deadline that would come before the proposal can run, an upgrade that wouldn't leave room for a `CancelUpgrade` after it runs, an upgrade to a version a cancellation withdrew, a cancellation of an upgrade that isn't scheduled (yet) or that it couldn't reach 64 blocks before the activation, a distribution id a distribution already has, a safety parameter outside its band. Accounts named by `.boo` name are resolved to addresses when the proposal is made, so the account voters see is the account the proposal acts on, even if the name changes hands.

```
created_at ─── voting ───► voting_end ─── timelock ───► execute_at
             Vote txs         tally,         queued         runs at the end
             (through         deposit                       of that block
             voting_end)      settled
```

At the end of block `voting_end` the vote closes:

- **Quorum:** the raw FANG voted (ayes + nays, before conviction) must reach `max(quorum_floor, quorum_bps × unlocked supply)`. The unlocked supply is what could have voted: the FANG held by every account except the keyless system accounts (treasury, community, endowment, bootstrap delegation, contribution pool; the bootstrap delegation's stake in validator pools doesn't count either), liquid, staked (each pool at its stake, so delegators' shares count at their exact pro-rata value) or unbonding, less what vesting locks still hold of each vesting account's holdings. Balances, pool stake and unbonding are running totals the state maps keep as they change, and only genesis allocations carry vesting locks, so it costs O(vesting accounts), never a walk over every account (`governance::unlocked_supply`; `check_invariants` compares it with a full walk). It's taken once an epoch, when the epoch's first vote closes, and every vote closing in that epoch uses that figure.
- **Majority** on conviction-weighted votes: ayes must outweigh nays, or, for a supermajority kind, make up at least `supermajority_bps` of the weight. Supermajority kinds: `ScheduleUpgrade` and `CancelUpgrade` (a simple majority can't undo what a supermajority scheduled; to block an upgrade, vote nay on its `ScheduleUpgrade`), `ActivateFeature` (a security-critical switch such as Ghost Mode; `DeactivateFeature` takes a simple majority), `ParamChange` of a parameter that's the token holders' alone (below: governance's safety parameters and the validators' own economics, including the `issuance_*` parameters, whose code bounds also keep the 0.5% floor and the stakers' 50%), and `TreasurySpend` above `treasury_large_bps` of the treasury's balance at the close, counted together with the spends that passed in the last 30 days (`LARGE_SPEND_WINDOW_DAYS`; ones that failed when they ran drop out), so a large spend split into small proposals still needs the supermajority (audit F44).
- **Bootstrap council** (until `bootstrap_end_height`): a proposal also passes if ⅔ of the active validator set at the close voted aye and the token-weighted nays don't exceed the token-weighted ayes. A validator's council vote is any `Vote` sent from its consensus key's account or its owner's account (`staking::validator_for_signer`); one per validator, the latest replaces earlier ones. The council is 22–31 independent operators with at most 2 founder seats, so no single party can pass anything. It passes upgrades, their cancellations and feature switches, every other kind, and `ParamChange`s of the keys listed in `params::COUNCIL_KEYS` (the active set's size, unbonding, fees, block size, the account creation fee, tx validity, the proposal deposit, multisig expiry, name prices and periods, Ghost Mode's limits). Every other parameter, including any added later and not listed, is the token holders' alone (`params::tokens_only`, `governance::council_may_pass`; `/api/proposal` shows `council_may_pass`): governance's safety parameters (below), so the council can't extend itself, open spending early or weaken the vote that checks it, and the validators' own economics (`params::VALIDATOR_KEYS`: the `issuance_*` parameters, `tip_cap_multiple`, `commission_max_bps` and `commission_max_change_bps`, `min_self_bond`, `insider_delegation_cap_bps`, `bootstrap_commission_cap_bps`, the `slash_*` parameters, `jail_blocks`, `downtime_window` and `downtime_max_missed`, `evidence_max_age_epochs`, `reporter_reward_bps`, `beacon_miss_slash_bps`), since the council is the validators (review-integrate-merges-1). A rejected change says why: "no token quorum (…), and the bootstrap council can't pass a change to KEY: it's the token holders' alone".
- **Safety parameters** (`params::GOVERNANCE_KEYS`: `voting_blocks`, `timelock_blocks`, `lock_period_blocks`, `quorum_bps`, `quorum_floor`, `supermajority_bps`, `treasury_large_bps`, `governance_spend_height`, `bootstrap_end_height`; audit F15, F16) set how hard the next vote is to win, so a change needs the supermajority, never passes by the council, and stays inside a band in code (`ChainParams::governance_band` and `check_governance_change`, checked when proposed and again when it runs): voting at least 3 days, the timelock and the lock period at least a day, quorum 1–10% of the unlocked supply and 5M–50M FANG, the supermajority 60–80%, the large-spend line at most 5% of the treasury. The band has a ceiling as well as a floor, so no single vote can make the next one impossible to win (a 100% supermajority would let one drop of nay veto every upgrade, its own repeal included; review-fix-gov-3), and whatever a vote sets, the next vote can set back. The spend gate never moves earlier, and later by at most a year past the block that moves it (`SPEND_GATE_MAX_DELAY_DAYS`), so spending reopens by itself; the council's end only moves earlier. The evidence window has a floor in code as well (`evidence_max_age_epochs` × `epoch_blocks` at least two weeks, `params::EVIDENCE_WINDOW_FLOOR_DAYS`, checked the same way when proposed and when it runs; a chain that starts below it, like `blockula dev`'s, only moves up): joiners' weak-subjectivity period is half the window, so it never drops below a week, the weekly signed checkpoint stays fresh enough, and since `unbonding_blocks` can't be shorter than the window, unbonding can't fall below two weeks either (audit gap-6-1; OPERATIONS.md "Weak subjectivity"). Mainnet's genesis must start inside every band and at or above that floor, with the spend gate at most a year out (`check_governance_floors`). `tip_cap_multiple` is one of the validators' own parameters (above): a higher cap lets one signature pay its block's proposer more (audit F01); its code bounds are 1–1,000. So is `bootstrap_commission_cap_bps` (10% at genesis, bounds 0–100%), the most commission the bootstrap delegation's stake pays a validator: raising it pays the operators who sit on the council more of the treasury's bootstrap rewards (audit F12).
- **Deposit:** refunded if the vote reached quorum, or if ⅔ of the council voted (either way); burned otherwise. That keeps spam expensive without punishing a proposal that was considered and voted down.

A passed proposal is queued and runs at the end of block `voting_end + timelock_blocks` (the timelock in force when its vote closes). A `CancelUpgrade` has no timelock and runs at the end of block `voting_end + 1`: the timelock gives whoever disagrees time to act before a change, and a cancellation changes nothing (the rules in force stay), while it has to land before the upgrade it stops. If its action fails then (say, the treasury can't cover it yet), nothing it did stays and it's marked failed, with the reason. At the end of a block, every vote that ended closes first, then the queued proposals whose time has come run (in id order): what a proposal sees when it runs (which others passed: a `CancelUpgrade` spends the numbers of the upgrades that passed) doesn't depend on proposal ids, and the votes closing in a block are tallied against the state before anything runs in it (review-r3-gov-2).

| Kind | Runs |
|---|---|

A handle validators have credited to another account (its first verified claim) isn't held: only that account can link it |
| `UnflagSybil { account, reason }` | `FlagSybil`'s appeal: lifts the flag (the account and its GitHub handle can earn again), restores nothing the flag took; only for a flagged account (`blockula gov propose unflag-sybil`) |

Too late for a vote: OPERATIONS.md "Upgrades" |
| `ActivateFeature`, `DeactivateFeature` | `upgrade.rs` (Ghost Mode switches on this way) |
| `Text {}` | nothing: a recorded signal |

Proposal ids count from 0. A distribution's id is the one its proposal names (`/api/distribution/{id}`'s `proposal` is the proposal that opened it).

## Votes

`Vote { proposal_id, aye, amount, conviction, proposal_digest }` (owner keys only) while the proposal is voting. `proposal_digest` is `types::proposal_digest` of the proposal's kind (accounts as stored), title and link: a vote signed for other content is refused, so a node can't attach one proposal's text to another's id (audit gap-7-6). Clients show what the digest covers, rendered from the kind (`describeKind`; `blockula gov show` prints it with the digest), never the node's `summary`, which nothing binds; the MCP's `vote` and `blockula gov vote --digest` refuse a proposal that reads differently from what was shown. `amount` can't exceed the voter's `econ::voting_balance`: unvested tokens don't vote, staked ones do. The vote locks `amount` with `econ::lock` (id `vote:<proposal>`) while the height is below `voting_end + 1 + periods × lock_period_blocks`:

| `conviction` | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| weight | 0.1× | 1× | 2× | 3× | 4× | 5× | 6× |
| lock periods after voting ends | 0 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 |

Even at conviction 0 the tokens stay locked until voting ends, so they can't vote twice from two accounts. Vote locks overlap each other: the same tokens may vote on several proposals at once. They come on top of what's still vesting (votes only use vested tokens, so a vesting account's voted tokens can't move and vote again elsewhere; audit F03), and a lock constrains transfers out (P-ECON), never staking. Voting again on the same proposal replaces the vote (and the council vote it carried); its lock only grows, to the larger amount and the later expiry, and only the growth must be held when it's cast, so a full-balance voter can re-vote after fees took a little (audit F42). `Unlock {}` releases expired locks (`econ::release_expired`).

Once a vote closes its tally is final, and its vote records are swept, oldest closed proposal first, `VOTE_SWEEP` (256) per block (audit F47): `/api/proposal` then reports `votes_swept` with the tally, and each voter's lock lives on in its account.

## Treasury and community distributions

The treasury and the community account are keyless system accounts. Only a passed `TreasurySpend` moves treasury funds, and only `Distribution` proposals move community funds. Both are refused before `governance_spend_height`, because at launch almost nothing can vote.

A distribution escrows `total` from the community account. Each recipient claims once with `ClaimDistribution { distribution_id, amount, proof }` until block `claim_until`, as the account the tree names (the tx's `from`); claims can't add up to more than `total`. At the end of the first block after `claim_until`, what's left goes back to the community account. A recipient with nothing to pay the fee with (a new or empty account) pays it out of the claim: admission counts the claim toward the fee once it checks out against the stored root (open, not claimed yet, the proof leads to `merkle_root`, enough left), the claim runs first in the fee phase, and a claim that opens the account also burns `account_creation_fee` from it (`governance::claim_toward_fee`). A claim that doesn't check out is no help, so from an empty account it isn't admitted at all: a bad proof is never free.

### Merkle tree format

- **Leaf:** `BLAKE3("BLOCKULA_DISTRIBUTION_LEAF_V1" ‖ distribution_id u64 LE ‖ len(address) u16 LE ‖ address ‖ amount u64 LE)`. `address` is the bech32m string as the claimant's tx spells it (lowercase, with the chain's prefix); `amount` is in drops.
- **Node:** `BLAKE3("BLOCKULA_DISTRIBUTION_NODE_V1" ‖ min(a, b) ‖ max(a, b))`, comparing the two 32-byte children as byte strings. Sorted pairs make a proof just the sibling hashes from the leaf up, with no positions; the two domain strings keep a node from passing as a leaf.
- **Building** (`governance::build_distribution`, `blockula gov tree`): sort the leaf hashes ascending, then pair them left to right level by level, carrying an odd last node up unchanged; one leaf is its own root. Addresses must be distinct and amounts non-zero. Any tree shape verifies; this one is the reference so independent builders agree on the root.
- **Verifying:** fold the proof: `h = leaf; for s in proof: h = node(h, s)`; the claim is valid if `h == merkle_root`. Proofs are at most 64 hashes (`MAX_PROOF`).
- **The id is in the leaf**, so a proof for one distribution never verifies in another. The proposer picks the id: build the tree for one no distribution has, at most 1,000 past the next proposal id (`max_distribution_id` in `/api/proposals`; the range only grows, so a tree built for an allowed id stays allowed), and name it in the kind: `Distribution { distribution_id, merkle_root, total, claim_until }`. `next_distribution_id` in `/api/proposals` (what `blockula gov tree` uses) is the smallest id at or above the number of distributions that no distribution, open `Distribution` proposal or multisig's stored one names, so an outlier id can't drag it anywhere (review-r2-gov-2). The node refuses a `Propose` whose id an opened distribution already has (the tx fails before any deposit is taken; `blockula gov propose distribution` checks before sending), and checks again when it runs: the first proposal naming an id to run opens it, a later one fails ("already exists"). Open proposals naming the same id don't block each other, so filing a proposal can't take an id away from another, and the id doesn't depend on which proposals land first: a multisig's `Distribution` proposal, which only reaches `Propose` at its last approval, survives other proposals landing during its approval window (review-fix-gov-2; audit F45). Distributions are never deleted, so ids stay unique and a leaf names one distribution.

## Multisig accounts

`MultisigCreate { owners, threshold }` makes the sending account M-of-N over its owner keys. `owners` must list exactly the account's owner keys (add or remove keys first), and `1 ≤ threshold ≤ owners`. From then on:

- The account's owner keys may only sign `MultisigPropose` and `MultisigApprove`; admission refuses any other tx they sign (`governance::check_multisig_admission`, called from `State::admit`). Session keys are unaffected and keep spending within their limits.
- `MultisigPropose { action }` records the action with the proposer's approval. `MultisigApprove { id, action_digest }` adds another owner key's; `action_digest` is `types::action_digest` of the stored action (BLAKE3 of its encoding), and an approval whose digest doesn't match is refused, so a node that shows a co-signer a routine payment while the stored action is a drain can't turn their approval into the drain (audit gap-7-6). `blockula multisig show` prints each pending action decoded, described (`describeAction` in the SDK) and with its digest; `approve --digest <digest>` refuses an action that reads differently from what `show` printed (a node can answer the two reads differently), and without it `approve` prints the action and asks before signing. The SDK's `multisigApprove(id, { action })` binds the action it was given (or reads). The tx that brings the approvals of current owner keys to `threshold` runs the action as the account (with an owner key's authority, paying from the account); if the action fails, that approval fails too and the proposal stays open, so it can be retried.
- Proposals expire after `multisig_expiry_blocks`; at most 32 are open per account, and each owner key may have proposed at most ⌊32 / owner keys⌋ of them (at least 1), so one rogue key can't fill the list (audit F14). Expired proposals are swept every block (`MULTISIG_SWEEP`, 32 per block, by a cursor in `governance::end_block`) and when an approval finds one, so the other keys can always propose and approve the rogue key's `RemoveKey`.
- A proposed action is at most `MAX_MULTISIG_ACTION_BYTES` (12 KiB) encoded, a format rule: a nested validator registration fits, 32 KiB of equivocation evidence doesn't (audit F17).
- The owner set is always the account's current owner keys: an approved `AddKey`/`RemoveKey` changes it, but never below `threshold`. A multisig changes its threshold by proposing `MultisigCreate` again.

Multisig accounts can propose, vote, register validators and do everything else an account can, through their approvals. The foundation's allocation (DEC-5) is meant to sit in one with at least two non-founder signers. A validator a multisig owns keeps signing its system txs with its consensus key (SF-13), but its terms (commission and addresses, `UpdateValidator`) and `Unjail` go through the multisig's approvals only: the consensus key can't change them for the multisig, nor from the validator's own account (audit review-r2-integrate-compose-1).

## What replaced the admin levers

- **GOV-2 (Hunts):** only a hunt's creator can award it, on every chain. Nobody can redirect escrow: cancelling (the creator after expiry, an admin on dev and test chains, or a `RefundHunt` proposal) refunds every funder exactly.
- **GOV-3 (Proof of Contribution):** Soul starts from the published genesis `soul_ledger`; afterwards only juries and `GrantSoul` proposals (≤ 100 Soul each) change it. `SetRepo`, `GrantSoul`, `FlagSybil`, `RejectClaim` and `Clawback` as txs work only on chains with admins (dev and test); elsewhere they fail with "this chain has none: put it to a governance proposal". Mainnet's genesis has no admins (DEC-10).

## State and interfaces

- Records (`state::ns`): proposals (37), votes keyed `(proposal, voter)` (38), multisigs (39), multisig proposals (40), distributions (41), claims keyed `(distribution, address)` (42); the META scalar `governance` holds the id counters, the open proposal and distribution lists `end_block` walks, the epoch's unlocked supply, the treasury spends of the last 30 days, the closed proposals whose votes are being swept and the multisig sweep cursor. A proposal's kind and a multisig proposal's action are stored in their tx encoding.
- `State::check_invariants` covers deposits of proposals still voting and what's left in open distributions as escrow, tallies of proposals still voting as the sum of their votes (a closed proposal's records left to sweep are part of its final tally), council votes matching their voters', multisigs keeping `threshold` owner keys, and distributions matching their claims.
- Other packages provide `econ::{voting_balance, transferable, lock, release_expired}` (vesting and vote locks), `staking::{holdings, validator_for_signer}` (stake and the council's members), and the lever bodies in `chain.rs`, `poc.rs` and `upgrade.rs`.
- HTTP: `/api/proposals`, `/api/proposal/{id}`, `/api/distribution/{id}?address=`, `/api/multisig/{account}`. CLI: `blockula gov list | show | propose <kind> | vote | unlock | tree | claim`, `blockula multisig create | propose | approve | show`.
